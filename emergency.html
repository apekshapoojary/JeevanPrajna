Suspected Heart Attack

Signs: Crushing chest heaviness, pain radiating to left shoulder/arm or jaw, cold sweating, nausea, sudden shortness of breath.

Immediate Action: Call 112. Have the person chew one 300mg soluble Aspirin/Disprin tablet while sitting upright.
Sudden Fall or Fracture

Signs: Severe pain in hip or leg, inability to stand, one leg appearing shorter or turned outward.

Immediate Action: Do NOT force the person to stand. Keep warm with a blanket. Call 108 or 112.
Suspected Stroke (FAST)

Signs: Face drooping, Arm drift, Slurred speech. Golden treatment window is 4.5 hours.

Immediate Action: Call 112 immediately. Note the exact time symptoms began. Do NOT give water or aspirin.

Evidence-Based First Aid

Critical First-Aid Actions While Waiting for the Ambulance

Standard operating procedures approved by emergency physicians and cardiologists. Knowing these steps saves lives in the critical first 15 minutes.

01

Suspected Heart Attack Protocol

Time is Muscle — Act Fast
  1. Dial 112 / 108 immediately: State clearly that an elderly person is having severe chest pain.
  2. Chew 300mg Soluble Aspirin (Disprin): If the patient is conscious and not allergic to aspirin, have them chew one 300mg Disprin tablet immediately. Chewing allows rapid oral mucous absorption within 5 minutes, inhibiting blood clots.
  3. Keep Seated Upright: Do NOT let the patient lie flat, as this increases venous return to an already struggling heart and worsens breathlessness. Seat them comfortably supported against a wall or sofa.
  4. Loosen Clothing: Open tight collars, belts, or sarees to assist effortless breathing.
  5. Prepare for CPR if Unresponsive: If the patient collapses, loses consciousness, and stops breathing normally, begin continuous chest compressions at 100–120 beats per minute in the centre of the chest until the ambulance arrives.
02

Suspected Brain Stroke (FAST Protocol)

Golden Window: 4.5 Hours
  1. F — Face Drooping: Ask the person to smile. Does one side of their mouth droop down?
  2. A — Arm Weakness: Ask the person to raise both arms out forward. Does one arm drift downward or feel completely numb?
  3. S — Speech Difficulty: Ask them to repeat a simple sentence like "Today is a sunny day." Is speech slurred, strange, or unable to find words?
  4. T — Time to Call 112: Call an emergency ambulance immediately. Note the exact minute symptoms began.
  5. CRITICAL WARNING: Never give food, water, or blood thinners (like Aspirin) to a stroke patient! If the stroke is caused by a ruptured blood vessel (haemorrhage), aspirin can be fatal. Keep their head slightly elevated.
03

Senior Fall & Trauma Management

Prevent Spinal & Hip Aggravation
  1. Stay Calm & Check for Consciousness: Kneel next to the person. Ask: "Are you hurt? Where does it pain?"
  2. Do NOT Rush to Pull Them Up: Forcing a senior with a fractured hip or neck trauma to stand can tear femoral blood vessels or damage the spinal cord.
  3. Suspected Fracture Signs: Severe pain in the groin/hip, inability to bear any weight, or a foot turned outward indicates a hip fracture. Keep them warm and call 112/108.
  4. If Completely Uninjured: Guide them to turn onto their side, move onto their hands and knees, crawl to a sturdy chair or bed, place both hands on the seat, bring one foot forward flat on the floor, and slowly push up to sit on the chair.
  5. Medical Review: Even if no bones appear broken, visit a clinic. Head bumps in seniors taking blood thinners require a CT scan to rule out slow internal bleeds (subdural haematoma).
04

Severe Hypoglycemia (Low Blood Sugar)

15-15 Protocol
  1. Recognise Warning Signs: Sudden trembling hands, profuse cold sweating, pale skin, extreme hunger, irritability, confusion, or slurred speech in a diabetic senior.
  2. Administer 15 Grams of Fast Sugar: If conscious, immediately give:
    • 3 teaspoons of table sugar dissolved in 1/2 cup of water, OR
    • Half glass of sweet fruit juice (frooti, orange juice), OR
    • 4 to 5 glucose tablets or sweet candies.
  3. Wait 15 Minutes: Recheck blood sugar with a glucometer. If still below 70 mg/dL, repeat with another 15g of sugar.
  4. Follow with a Complex Snack: Once blood sugar rises above 80 mg/dL, give a slice of bread, a roti, or a glass of milk to prevent a second drop.
  5. If Unconscious: NEVER pour liquids into the mouth of an unconscious senior (choking risk). Place them on their side in the recovery position and call 112 immediately.
