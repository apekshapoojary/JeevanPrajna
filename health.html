Clinical Pharmacology Rules

7 Golden Rules of Safe Medication in Seniors

Aging alters liver metabolism and kidney clearance. Follow these evidence-backed safeguards to avoid adverse drug reactions and dangerous drug interactions.

01

Never Stop Heart or BP Pills Abruptly

Suddenly stopping BP tablets like Telmisartan or Beta-blockers causes dangerous rebound hypertension and triggers stroke risk. Always consult your doctor before modifying doses.

02

Strict Timing for Thyroid & Acidity Tablets

Levothyroxine (Thyronorm) must be taken alone with plain water at least 45 minutes before morning tea or milk. Never take Calcium or Iron pills with thyroid medicine (keep a 4-hour gap).

03

Beware of OTC Painkillers (NSAIDs)

Over-the-counter painkillers like Combiflam, Brufen, and Diclofenac can cause rapid kidney damage, worsen blood pressure, and cause stomach ulcers in seniors. Prefer safe Paracetamol (Dolo 650) for joint pain.

04

Use a 7-Day AM/PM Pill Dispenser Box

Accidental double-dosing or forgotten pills are the #1 cause of emergency hospital visits in older adults. A simple compartmental pill organizer with morning, noon, and night slots eliminates confusion.

05

Know the 15-15 Rule for Low Blood Sugar

If a senior taking diabetes medicines feels shaky, sweaty, confused, or unusually drowsy, immediately give 15 grams of fast-acting sugar (3 teaspoons of sugar dissolved in water, or fruit juice), wait 15 minutes, and retest.

06

Monitor Kidney Function (Creatinine / eGFR)

Many common drugs (Metformin, BP medicines) require lower doses if kidney clearance decreases. Seniors with diabetes or hypertension should have a Serum Creatinine and eGFR test performed every 6 months.

07

Inform Doctors About All Supplements & Ayurvedic Formulations

Ayurvedic powders, herbal formulations, and unlabelled churnas can interact with blood thinners and strain liver function. Always carry the exact packaging to your physician.

ICMR Dietary Targets for Indian Seniors

  • Protein is Priority: Aim for 1.0 to 1.2 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight daily (e.g. 60g for a 60kg adult) to prevent muscle wasting (sarcopenia). Include curd, paneer, cooked dal, eggs, sattu, or sprouts.
  • Hydration Awareness: The thirst sensation naturally weakens with age. Drink at least 1.5 to 2 litres (6–8 glasses) of clean water daily unless restricted by your kidney or heart specialist.
  • Dietary Salt Limit: Keep salt under 5 grams (1 teaspoon) per day across all curries, dals, and pickles to protect vascular elasticity and prevent fluid buildup.
  • Calcium & Sun Exposure: 15 minutes of early morning sun exposure helps synthesize natural Vitamin D for bone density and joint cushioning.
Household Printable Tools

Senior Healthcare Practical Checklists

Downloadable, clean printable templates formatted to fit in a wallet or hang on the refrigerator door.

Doctor Visit Preparation Sheet

Doctors often have only 10–15 minutes per consultation. Using a structured sheet ensures no critical symptom or drug reaction is missed.

  • List of top 3 primary complaints in order of discomfort
  • Complete log of all current daily pills with brand and dosage
  • Recent morning blood pressure readings and fasting sugar log
  • Any new allergies, digestion issues, or recent dizzy episodes

Senior Emergency Pocket Card

A pocket card carried in a wallet or handbag saves vital minutes if an elderly family member feels faint or has an accident away from home.
